01 01 — Preface

Before anything else

I think everyone who works with music waits for a brief like this one. A chance to combine what we need to say with what we want to say. We need to show the world what Studio 2.0 can do. But what we want to say is much bigger: that the process of making music — the years, the failures, the accidents, the people — is worth protecting.

The conversation around AI and music right now is loud, anxious, and often reductive. This campaign is our answer to it. Not defensive, not nostalgic — a declaration of love for the way music is actually made, told by the people who make it.

The treatment below was written with that feeling inside me. Thank you for the opportunity. Let's make it happen.

Let's go.

02 02 — The Premise

Every Moises film is a film about the process

In the end, that is the heart of everything we produce. Every editorial campaign Moises makes is built on the same foundation — and these are not guidelines. They are convictions.

Every piece of content starts from the same premise: real musicians, creating, playing, and recording real music — collaborating with each other, in real environments, with real stakes. Moises is a facilitator. It removes technical barriers. It does not eliminate the creative or technical process.

♩

Real performance

Photoshoots are made with real musicians performing and recording. The sound is genuinely captured. Artists prepare, rehearse, and show up — like a real gig.

♪

Real soundtracks

Every video involves the composition of an original soundtrack. The full arc of making music is present: research → inspiration → conception → recording → finalization → mixing.

♫

Real people

Characters are real humans, openly sharing their creative process, their inspirations, and the life stories behind their careers. Those stories determine the sound of each piece.

03 03 — Why It Matters

The emotional case

Why do these premises matter — and why will they matter to people? Six reasons, each one a pillar of the campaign.

A — Catalyst, not eliminator

Technology should expand creative possibility, not replace creative humanity. We are here to strengthen the music ecosystem — not erase it.

B — The ecosystem lives

Technicians, engineers, producers, session musicians, studios, equipment makers, live crews — the full human ecosystem of music is alive and present in everything we make.

C — The process is irreplaceable

The moment an artist discovers a sound they didn't expect. The frustration before the breakthrough. The joy of finishing something that didn't exist before. Machines cannot replicate this — and it is exactly what we document and celebrate.

D — Flow: challenge becomes fulfillment

When a musician pushes through complexity and arrives at a moment of clarity and expression — that is flow. That state of deep, effortful engagement is where music becomes meaningful. The process is not a burden to be automated away. It is the source of everything that makes music worth feeling.

E — Music as identity

For artists who dedicate their lives to music, creating is not just professional — it is existential. The years of practice, the teachers, the failures, the breakthroughs: that path gives their music weight and truth. Protecting it is not nostalgic. It is necessary.

F — Global, plural, rooted

By working with real characters from diverse origins, Moises belongs to the whole world — not one scene, one genre, or one market. Every artist we feature carries a cultural story worth telling. Different worlds — one music.

If creating a song is one click of a generic button, you are not making music. You are listening to a personalized stream of a song tailored to you. It will never bring the joy of actually playing.
04 04 — The Concept

Music has always been born from collision

Music does not evolve in isolation. Every great genre, every defining sound, every cultural movement in music history was born from the meeting — the collision — of different influences, backgrounds, and human experiences.

This is not a theory. It is the history of music itself:

Jazz

New Orleans

West African rhythm × blues × European harmony × the lived experience of Black Americans navigating a fractured society. The most influential music America ever produced.

Rock & Roll

United States

Blues × gospel × country × rhythm & blues — cultural currents meeting at one historical moment, creating something that changed the world.

Bossa Nova

Rio de Janeiro, late 1950s

Brazilian samba, with deep African roots × American cool jazz — filtered through the sensibility of Rio. One of the most sophisticated musical forms ever created.

Hip-Hop

South Bronx

Funk × soul × Caribbean sound-system culture × a generation with limited resources and unlimited creative will. DJ-ing, sampling and MC-ing became a new musical language.

Reggaeton

Panama & Puerto Rico

Jamaican dancehall × American hip-hop × Latin Caribbean rhythms — born local, grown into a global phenomenon.

Afrobeats

West Africa

Traditional West African rhythms × highlife × funk × R&B × electronic production — simultaneously ancient and completely of this moment.

The creative decision behind every note is never just a sound. It is the manifestation of everything an artist has lived, learned, and inherited — their culture, their community, their masters, their language. When an artist creates something inspired by music they love, that is already a collaboration: our musical vocabulary was built by relating to the creations of those who came before us.

We are always reinterpreting the past with the vision of our present. Music is a continuous conversation — across generations, across continents, across time, into the future.

When different worlds collide, new music is born.
Collaboration never ends.
05 05 — Our Position

The artist at the center. Always.

AI is changing music. But the creative process won't be cut. Our position, stated plainly:

No one-click songs
We don't want to generate an entire song with one click — this is boring. The process is not an obstacle. The process is the point.
Facilitate, don't kill
We remove technical barriers, but we don't kill the process. A certain amount of steps — a bit of complexity — is still good: it opens space for creative accidents, more time to explore, to experiment, to go deeper, to relate to each other. Not to skip the journey, but to go further into it.
Meaning through the path
The path the artist travels through the process creates meaning for the song and fulfillment for the artist.
Honor the ecosystem
Technicians, engineers, producers, session musicians, studio crews, live crews, equipment makers — every human role that makes music a living, breathing industry.
Celebrate every era
Every instrument, every piece of gear, every era of music-making. Nothing is destroyed. Everything is honored.
Cultural originality
Traditional rhythms, regional scenes, classic genres, and the movements that shaped the world's musical identity are not relics — they are living creative forces. The collision between these backgrounds is what brings originality to musical expression.

07 07 — The Manifesto Film

Ninety seconds, drawn by hand

The campaign opens with a 90-second manifesto film — the voiceover narration told through hand-drawn illustration: charcoal textures, gesso strokes, ink outlines. Raw, expressive, imperfect — human marks on a surface, emerging from darkness.

Near-monochromatic: deep blacks and off-whites, with a single teal accent appearing only at key emotional moments. Illustrations animate subtly, like a drawing completed in real time. One drawing dissolves or morphs into the next — lines transforming, never cutting.

Tone of narration: deeply human, warm, cinematic. Pace: slow — let silence do its work. A manifesto told like a story.

08 08 — The Campaign

Three phases, one crescendo

The campaign builds like a song: first the individual voice, then the collaboration, then the room where everyone meets. Every phase keeps the same premise — involve and explore the creative process of musicians in action.

01

Show the Creative Process

Up to 9 films

We invite 3–4 real musicians to create a track using Moises Studio 2.0, each exploring the features in their own genre and creative language. We film the full creative process — each artist explains what they do and how the tool fits naturally into how they already work.

Each film begins with a musical idea — a riff — and follows its development, showing how the new product features are used throughout songwriting and production. The initial input can come from another artist's file: collaboration embodied from the very first note, because learning from another's work is itself creative dialogue.

Central message: the product facilitates — it does not kill the process. The human creative decisions, the exploration, the moments of doubt and discovery remain entirely the artist's.

02

Artist Collaboration

12 films · launches September

We bring together 3 artists per track — from different cities, countries, and musical worlds: a lead artist, a producer, and session musicians, each from a different cultural background. By colliding characters from different origins and genres, we honor cultural differences and celebrate what makes each story unique — traditional rhythms, local scenes, genre-defining movements.

Three tracks, each highlighting musical elements and genres from different continents. The campaign is global — North America, Europe, Brazil, China — because music belongs to no single place.

The Showcase

Live event · TBD

A live pocket show where the artists perform the track they created together — for a curated audience of producers, labels, and industry partners. Professionally filmed and released online, then repurposed as editorial content.
